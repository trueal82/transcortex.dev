Six tasks where an LLM can help

Extract information

Transfer quantities, product features, or contact details from text into defined fields. Scanned documents may also need text recognition.

Benefit: Less manual entry. Missing or conflicting details are flagged for clarification.

Route enquiries

Categorise a message as a product question, complaint, or quote request and prepare the appropriate handling.

Benefit: Less sorting work. Ambiguous classifications have a review path.

Prepare drafts

Draft replies, product descriptions, or explanatory quote text using approved facts and templates.

Benefit: An editable starting point. Facts and commitments are checked before sending.

Summarise cases

Turn long email threads or case notes into a concise overview with open questions and next steps.

Benefit: Get up to speed sooner. Original documents remain available for checking.

Draw on relevant knowledge

Retrieve relevant sections from approved manuals or internal documents and use them to draft a response. This approach is often called RAG.

Benefit: Less searching. Access rights, freshness, and source references are part of implementation; sourced answers still need checking.

Standardise terminology

Map different supplier descriptions to a suggested term or an existing category.

Benefit: Preparation for consistent product data. Critical attributes and product matches are validated.

Language models, rules, and approvals work together

Fixed rules for well-defined tasks

Calculations, required fields, prices, permissions, and exact record matching belong in explicit logic or the authoritative system. A straightforward data sync often needs no LLM.

Checks for model responses

LLMs can misinterpret details or invent information. We validate output formats and values, check important details against sources, and route unclear cases for review. A correctly formatted response can still be factually wrong.

Incoming documents are treated as data. Instructions within them should not change permissions or business rules. If a model can take actions through tools, we limit those actions and define appropriate approvals.
