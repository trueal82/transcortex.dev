Use cases

Four typical SME situations, the way they come up in practice. These are illustrative examples, not anonymized client references — as soon as real projects exist, real numbers will stand here.

Invoice intake

The situation: Invoices arrive by email and as PDFs — and someone types the data into the accounting system by hand. Or worse: into a form at the tax office.

The solution: An n8n workflow picks up every incoming invoice, a language model extracts vendor, amount, date, and line items, a validation rule checks the result for plausibility, and the record lands cleanly structured in the accounting system.

The outcome: Minutes instead of days of processing time, far fewer transcription errors — and accounting verifies instead of keying in.

Email triage

The situation: A shared inbox that everyone reads. Customer inquiries, invoices, newsletters, spam — every message gets checked by everyone.

The solution: An LLM classifies every incoming email, assigns it to the right case, routes it to the right person, and drafts a reply for the standard cases, ready for approval.

The outcome: Your people only read what actually needs their attention — and answers to standard inquiries go out in minutes instead of the next day.

Quote and report generation

The situation: Quotes and reports are written by hand from CRM and ERP data — copy-paste from three systems, every week anew.

The solution: n8n aggregates the data from the source systems, a language model drafts the text to your specifications, a human reviews and sends.

The outcome: Hours turn into minutes, and the quality is consistent instead of depending on whoever had time that day.

CRM and system sync

The situation: Tool accounting, contact management, and the CRM slowly drift apart. Every record exists three times; no version is correct.

The solution: n8n keeps the systems in sync through its 400+ integrations, and an LLM enriches and cleans records on the way — detecting duplicates, normalizing addresses, fixing categories.

The outcome: One maintained data set instead of three half-finished ones — and nobody types the same customer in for the fourth time.
